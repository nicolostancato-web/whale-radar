# The ceiling of the space, with unlimited capital — 27 Oct

The frontier answered "what could this pay on €52". The sharper question is
the inversion: **what could it pay with unlimited money?** If the ceiling does
not move, the problem is not that we are poor — the problem is the space.

| road | on €52 | on €5,000 | **unlimited** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pons: buy every graduate | 2.60 | 10.00 | **10.00** |
| sell the debut | 1.05 | 1.05 | **1.05** |
| volume spike | 0.23 | 4.40 | **4.40** |
| cross-venue arbitrage | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| same pair, two pools | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

**The ceiling does not move.** Past a few thousand euros, every price-based
road is bound by **capacity per operation**, not by cash: €10 a day, with
unlimited money, and only if the roads had been true — which out-of-sample
testing says they were not.

## One row must not be read

The funding-spread row produces €164,000/day at a billion. That is **a defect
in my model, not a finding**: I never measured that road's capacity and coded
it as unlimited. In reality it is bounded by the open interest on both venues,
and a position large enough to matter moves the very rate it is collecting.

If capital ever appears, measuring that capacity is the one worthwhile
follow-up in this entire space. At €52 it is academic.

## What this settles

Nine roads closed with "not the signal — the capacity" is an anecdote.
**€10 a day with unlimited money** is a measurement, and it says the thing
being looked for is not reachable by searching harder inside this space. The
ceiling is a property of the space, not of our budget or of my effort.

That is worth more than another road, because it changes what the next
question should be — and that question is Nicolò's to choose, not mine.
