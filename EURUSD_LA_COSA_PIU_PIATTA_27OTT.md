# EUR/USD, the class test it deserved — 27 Oct

On 25 Oct I closed EUR/USD after **six rules**, then ran the class test on
**crypto** and extended the conclusion here without redoing it. Nicolò asked
about EUR/USD specifically. Remedied.

88 paired runs at hourly, 80 at five-minute, 54 at daily. Each rule against the
identical signals at shuffled times. No cost in the statistic.

| frequency | real rules | shuffled | extremes (|t|>2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hourly, 2 years** | median 0.00, tails ±1.03 | 0.00, ±1.13 | **2 vs 1** |
| **daily, 10 years** | 0.00, ±0.99 | 0.00, ±0.91 | **0 vs 0** |
| 5-minute, 2 months | 0.00, ±2.90 | 0.00, ±1.50 | 22 vs 2 |

## What the first two rows say

**At hourly and daily, real rules are indistinguishable from random entries —
not only in the centre but in the tails.** Across ten years of daily data there
is not one extreme value, in either the real set or the fake one.

This is stronger than anything measured elsewhere. On crypto the rules at least
**selected eventful moments** and produced fat tails. On EUR/USD they do not
even do that: it is **the flattest thing we have measured**.

Only at five minutes do the fat tails appear — 22 extremes against 2 — while
the centre stays at zero. There, the only thing the rules manufacture is
**false positives, eleven times the rate of chance**.

## The answer to the question asked

It is not that we failed to find the right rule on EUR/USD. At the frequencies
we could trade, that market behaves **exactly like a coin toss** — now measured
on its own data rather than inferred from another market.

Depth register: EUR/USD moves from **type B (six rules)** to **class-measured**.
