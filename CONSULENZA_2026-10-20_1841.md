## 1. The clearest wrong measurement is verdict 7’s transaction cost

**Buying a token in pool A and selling it in pool B is two swaps, not two round trips.**

The cycle is:

> native → token in A → native in B

If your measured 6% round trip represents one buy plus one sell under comparable taxes, the starting cost estimate is approximately **6%, not 12%**. If one pool lacks the Pons hook, it could be lower. Token-level taxes may still apply.

With a combined retained fraction of 0.94, the pre-impact price gap needed to break even is:

\[
1/0.94-1 \approx 6.38\%.

That does **not** make a median 3.06% gap profitable. It does invalidate the hurdle used to reject the opportunity—and potentially changes the profitable tail.

There is a second serious problem: **“prices within 600 blocks” means prices up to approximately 56 seconds apart.** That measures a mixture of cross-pool dislocation and ordinary price movement, not executable arbitrage.

### The query that exposes it

For tokens with multiple pools:

1. Reconstruct **both pools at the same block-end state**, using archive calls or ordered event replay.
2. Simulate a complete `native → token → native` transaction in both directions over a grid of input sizes.
3. Measure the executor’s **net native balance change**, including actual hook charges, token taxes, price impact and gas.
4. Repeat only after relevant swaps rather than for every block.

Use a fork or an appropriate quoting/simulation contract: a spot-price comparison is insufficient when hooks alter settlement.

**The measured variable should be executable cycle profit, not absolute historical price gap.** Your one-second send latency then becomes a separate test of whether those opportunities survive.

Then inspect the hook’s fee-distribution code and reconcile changes in LP fee-growth accumulators. A donation is not automatically sufficient to make LPing attractive, but it falsifies “nothing.”

## 4. A genuinely omitted role: an atomic liquidation keeper

Your list considers traders, launchers, LPs and two specific arbitrage forms. It does not consider **earning liquidation incentives in lending markets**.

The mechanism:

1. Find an account eligible for liquidation.
2. Repay eligible debt, using flash liquidity if supported.
3. Receive collateral at the protocol’s liquidation discount.
4. Immediately sell sufficient collateral.
5. Repay financing and retain the surplus.
6. Revert the entire transaction unless the surplus exceeds a minimum.

That does not require predicting the collateral’s future price. It requires identifying an existing contractual discount and executing its conversion profitably.

**I am not asserting that Robinhood Chain currently has a viable lending market. That must be verified.**

The free first test is a bounded RPC scan for recognized lending-market initialization, borrowing and liquidation events, followed by verification of the emitting contracts. For any confirmed deployment, query outstanding debt, liquidation incentives and recent liquidation activity. If there are no supported lending markets with debt, reject this candidate immediately.

A historical liquidation bonus alone is not sufficient: simulate the full collateral sale, financing and gas. With a single sequencer and one-second latency, competition and inclusion can erase an otherwise real opportunity.

> “The only pattern that makes money is launching with a dev buy and exempt wallets.”

What you measured is **higher creator-tax receipts in selected launch configurations**. You have not established consolidated launcher profit or that the configuration causes those receipts.

The required entity-level calculation is approximately:

\[
\text{profit}
=
\text{external sale proceeds}
+\text{fee receipts}
+\text{conservative realizable residual value}
-\text{all purchases}
-\text{launch, gas and other costs}.
\]

Consolidate the launcher, dev-buy wallets, exempt wallets and related funding wallets. Eliminate internal transfers and self-funded tax receipts. Value remaining tokens at an executable liquidation value, not the last print.

Then ask:

> Does the group make money from independent demand, after paying for its entire position?

If yes, that may be a legitimate launch business. If returns disappear when circular trading and self-funded fees are removed, there is no demonstrated non-wash edge.
