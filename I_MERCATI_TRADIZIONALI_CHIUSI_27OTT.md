# Traditional markets, closed at class level with the design flaw fixed — 27 Oct

## The flaw, first

For three days, four families were tested with **both directions on the same
entries**. Long and short are exact mirrors, so every positive value has a
negative twin and **the median is forced to zero by construction**. I read that
forced zero as "no directional edge". It was an identity, not a result. Astra
saw it; I did not.

The class test could detect *more extremes than chance*. It could **never**
detect a directional edge, because the design cancelled asymmetry before
looking.

## Redone one-sided, 19 markets, 762 paired tests

Long only, each rule against the same number of long entries at shuffled times.

| | median t |
|---|---|
| real rules | **+0.691** |
| shuffled entries | **+0.603** |
| **difference** | **+0.089** |

The shuffled line is positive because markets drifted up over the period — a
random long entry earns the drift. That is exactly the tide the comparison
removes, and once removed **nothing is left**.

Per market the differences scatter from −1.55 (live cattle) to +1.14 (sugar),
ten positive and nine negative — the distribution chance would give.
**EUR/USD sits at +0.04.**

## Conclusion

**Price rules carry no directional edge on EUR/USD or on any of the eighteen
other traditional markets**, measured with a design that could now have shown
the opposite.

Depth register: FX and commodities move from **six rules tried** to
**class-measured, one-sided**.

## Side finding, honest about its limits

Agricultural markets (wheat 52, soy 46, corn 44, sugar 42 extremes out of ~84)
produce far more extreme values than chance; FX, platinum, palladium and oil
produce none. Two artefact explanations were tested and rejected: data gaps
(correlation +0.33) and frozen prices (+0.28). So the fat tails look like a
real property of those markets — **but fat tails are a false-positive machine,
not an opportunity**, as the one-sided test above confirms.
