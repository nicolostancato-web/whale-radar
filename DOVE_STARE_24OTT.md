# Where to stand — 24 Oct, the end of what measurement can answer

## The two chains, same question

> Who routes the flow, and could we take a share of it?

| | routing contracts | top 3 | needed for 90% of volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robinhood Chain | **1** | 99.7% | **1** |
| **Base** | **223** | 65.2% | **19** |

On Robinhood the toll side is shut for a reason none of the other fifteen roads
had: **the chain is the app.** The house routes everything; users are inside it,
not on the open web.

On Base the routing market is **contestable**. Two hundred and twenty-three
contracts compete; nineteen are needed to reach 90%.

## The number that replaces 962% a day

Base's evaluated flow is roughly **EUR 756M a day**, so an app charging 0.15%
across all of it would collect **~EUR 1.1M a day**.

> **EUR 500/day = 0.045% of Base's flow.**

Four hundredths of one percent. It is the smallest target we have had in three
months, and unlike every other one it needs **neither capital nor speed**.

It needs users. And that is where measurement stops being the useful tool.

## What three months bought, stated plainly

Not a strategy. A conversion:

| the goal, stated as | required |
|---|---|
| a return on EUR 52 | **962% per day** — 15,000× the best rate measured |
| capital earning the best measured yield | **EUR 787,000** |
| **a share of the flow, at a 0.15% toll** | **0.045% of Base** |

Same EUR 500 a day. Three different problems. Only the third is open to someone
with a laptop, no relationships and no inventory — and it is the one the
instrument could never have found by looking at prices, because it is not a
price.
