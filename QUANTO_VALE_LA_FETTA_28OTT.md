# How much the unguarded slice is worth — 28 Oct, 72% coverage

43 effective days, 192 positive occasions.

| winner type | value | per day |
|---|---|---|
| repeat players (3+ occasions, 27 addresses) | €53,284 — **15%** | €1,240 |
| **occasionals** | €296,232 — **85%** | **€6,896** |
| appearing **exactly once** (28 occasions) | €128,027 | €2,981 |

**The largest premium in the entire dataset — €98,150 — was taken by an
address that appears exactly once.**

Read naively, this says the big money goes to non-professionals, and a
systematic newcomer could contest €2,000–7,000 a day.

## The alternative explanation, which is not unlikely

**Professionals deploy a fresh contract for each operation.** It is standard
practice — it avoids being tracked and front-run. If that is what is
happening, the "50 occasionals" are a handful of actors behind many
throwaway contracts, and the unguarded slice does not exist.

This is exactly the shape of error already paid for twice today: the reading
that favours me, arriving first, needing a number before it is believed.

**The test:** follow the funding of those one-off contracts. Throwaway
contracts of one actor share a funder, or are deployed by the same address.
If the one-off winners cluster onto a few funders, the slice collapses; if
they are genuinely unrelated, it is real.

Nothing optimistic gets said until that runs.
