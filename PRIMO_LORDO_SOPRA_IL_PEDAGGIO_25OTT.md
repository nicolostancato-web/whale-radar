# First time the gross exceeds the toll — 25 Oct

Seventh family: **attention**, not price. Volume and trade count per hour — both
were already inside the candle response and I had been discarding them.

116 markets, 580,209 hours. Calibration exact in all six measurements (fifteen
in a row now): both directions of each rule average to −0.094%, the declared
cost.

## The gross signal, fading the move after an activity spike

| | 4h | 12h | 24h |
|---|---|---|---|
| trade-count spike | **+0.103%** | +0.044% | −0.086% |
| volume spike | **+0.121%** | +0.033% | +0.044% |

At four hours the gross is **0.10–0.12% against a toll of 0.094%**.

**This is the first time in the whole search that the inequality flips.** Six
families died because gross < toll. Here, briefly, gross > toll.

And it decays with horizon, which is the shape an overreaction that unwinds
would have.

## The net is not demonstrated

Fading after a volume spike at 4h: **+0.0266% ± 0.0438**. Positive, interval
contains zero.

Stated honestly: twelve tests were run here, and one at +0.027% with that
interval is unremarkable on its own. **The notable part is the gross, not the
net.**

## What would settle it

The interval must halve, which needs **four times the observations**: 17,713 →
~70,000. Available: 178 markets (used 116) and more history than the single
year taken.

No new idea required. Only more data.
