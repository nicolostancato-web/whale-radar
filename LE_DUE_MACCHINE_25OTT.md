# Two families, both empty at the centre, both wide at the edges — 25 Oct

The class test applied to the second family. Same design: rules generated
systematically, each paired against the identical signals at **shuffled times**,
cost excluded so the statistic measures signal.

| | median t | 5% | 95% | extremes (|t|>2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| attention rules | −0.22 | −3.02 | +3.02 | **27** of 76 |
| shuffled | −0.37 | −1.71 | +1.45 | **4** of 76 |

Centres coincide — no directional edge — and the real distribution is nearly
**seven times richer in extremes**.

That settles tonight's candidate: with 27 extremes of 76 **by construction**,
finding one that looked good was not luck, it was inevitable.

## Both class tests together — the most useful result of the search

| family | real extremes | chance extremes | multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| price patterns | 31/73 | 13/73 | 2.4× |
| **attention** | **27/76** | **4/76** | **6.8×** |

> **The better a selector is at finding eventful moments, the more false
> positives it produces — and therefore the more convincing its garbage looks.**

Attention produced our most persuasive candidate precisely because it is the
stronger illusion machine of the two. This is not a metaphor: two measured,
paired distributions over 477,180 hours.

## The operational consequence

When testing a family, never hunt for the winning rule. **Compare the whole
distribution against the same signals at shuffled times.** If the cloud is not
shifted, the family is empty and no individual rule inside it deserves a second
look, however good it appears — and the better it appears, the more suspicious
it should be.

Register: families 1, 2, 5 and 7 are now **class-measured**, not
six-rules-tried.
