# Three days out of twenty-five — 28 Oct

116 liquidations, 20% coverage of 60 days, 25 distinct days observed.

## The money is not spread out

| | |
|---|---|
| total value observed | €129,640 |
| **best single day (21 Sep)** | **€97,392 — 75% of everything** |
| best two days | 93% |
| best three days | **97%** |
| **median day** | **€1** |

This is not a daily job. **97% of the money arrives on three days out of
twenty-five**, and the other twenty-two pay one euro. The work is not
watching every day — it is being ready on the few days that matter.

## And those days are not technically harder

The obvious fear: the big days are market crashes, when every competitor is
awake and the network is congested, so competing costs most exactly when it
pays most. **The data says no.**

| | big day (21 Sep) | all other days |
|---|---|---|
| median gas | **0.425 gwei** | 0.157 gwei |
| maximum gas | 8.74 gwei | — |
| median position in block | **36** | 37 |

Gas was 2.7× normal — not a congestion event — and the position in block was
identical. Whatever caused the liquidations did not clog Ethereum.

(For contrast, 15 Sep shows a 124 gwei spike in one transaction, so spikes do
occur; they just did not occur on the day that paid.)

## What it means for the shape of the work

A liquidator is not a daily earner. It is an instrument that must be
**awake and correct on three days a month**, and on those days the technical
conditions are ordinary. That is a far more approachable profile than
"out-run professionals in a congested crisis", which is what I assumed.
