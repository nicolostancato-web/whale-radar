## 1. The weakest consequential verdict: “survives only at €50–200 per coin”

The measurements quoted do not establish that interval.

- Slippage at $50, $200 and above $5,000 does not establish a **€50 minimum**.
- Slippage generally becomes less troublesome at smaller sizes; fixed transaction costs become more troublesome. You need to measure their crossover.
- Those three points also do not establish €200 as the upper boundary.
- Entry slippage alone would not establish round-trip execution cost, especially when exits occur under different liquidity conditions.

**What would overturn the verdict:** an all-in cost and return measurement at **€2, €5 and €10**, including entry, exit, failed transactions, priority fees, unrecovered account costs, and unsellable positions. Use the same eligible coins and holding rules, not different cohorts for different sizes.

The economically important finding would be:

> A small ticket remains net-positive and permits a chronological, cash-constrained account to participate without repeatedly exhausting its available capital.

That would reopen the strategy as something compatible with €52. It would not yet prove profitability.

Do not choose whichever ticket produces the best historical return. Use a separate cost-calibration sample to choose one ticket size, then freeze it for the forward test.

**One additional inferential weakness:** +8.9 points against a +20-point threshold supports “failed our deployment hurdle.” Without uncertainty estimates, it does not establish “selection contains no useful signal.” I would nevertheless leave that closed for now: useful selection is downstream of establishing that the underlying strategy is executable.

## 2. Strongest reason the +18.9% will not translate forward: bankroll incompatibility

My strongest objection is not generic “markets change.” It is:

> **Buying every graduation may require simultaneous capital, while the reported average treats trades as if each had an independent allocation.**

At €50 a ticket, €52 buys approximately one position—not the next hundred opportunities. If that position takes days to reach 2x, you miss intervening coins. If it becomes stuck, your measurement correctly records a loss, but your real account also loses the ability to continue.

Consequently:

- Average return per coin is not account return.
- Average return does not reveal time to capital release.
- A positive arithmetic mean does not imply positive compounded growth.
- An edge distributed across many coins need not survive a capital-constrained subset.
- Correlated launches can make 334 coins much less than 334 independent observations.

“Compounding at +10% per coin” assumes that returns can be earned sequentially and capital recycled. **That is precisely what remains untested.**

### The cheap, pre-registered check

Run **two forward shadow ledgers on the same opportunities**:

| Ledger | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Unconstrained | Replicate the original strategy prospectively |
| €52 cash-constrained | Test whether you can actually operate it |

Pre-register:

**Eligibility and timing**
- The exact graduation definition and discovery source.
- Entry at the end of minute one, with a realistic detection and submission delay.
- Log every eligible opportunity before its outcome is known.
- No retrospective removal because data, a quote or a route became unavailable.

**Execution**
- Quotes for the actual ticket size, not chart prices.
- Exit at an executable quote satisfying the original 2x definition—not merely because a candle touched it.
- Include all previously unaccounted-for costs; do not subtract costs already included twice.
- An unavailable route is a failed attempt, not a missing observation.
- Quotes are evidence of executability, **not proof of fills**. Preserve that distinction.

**Capital**
- Start the constrained ledger with €52, including a declared fee reserve.
- Pick one ticket-sizing rule from the separate cost calibration.
- Never reuse capital while a position still holds it.
- When insufficient cash exists, skip according to a deterministic rule. Log the missed opportunity.
- No top-ups.

**Termination**
- Freeze the original maximum holding period. If none existed, the strategy is not fully specified; add one before testing.
- Keep every entered position through its prescribed outcome. At the deadline, apply the pre-registered terminal rule, including your −100% treatment of stuck positions.
- Do not select “common support” using future tradability. Failed exits must remain in the table.

**Sample and decision**
- Enrol opportunities for a fixed 14-day window, then wait for all positions to reach their prescribed endpoints.
- Primary outcome: terminal, spendable account equity after costs.
- Also report cash exhaustion, opportunities skipped, capital-lock duration and dependence on the largest winner.
- Treat this as a **cheap falsification screen**, not sufficient confirmation of an enduring edge.

If the unconstrained ledger wins and the €52 ledger fails, you have isolated the problem: **an opportunity-level edge without a viable bankroll policy**.

If both win, that earns a tightly capped execution experiment—not permission to compound the entire €52. Actual fill quality is still untested.

## 3. What earning category is missing?

The distinction matters: €52 is inadequate capital for many trading businesses, but potentially sufficient operating money for a small analytical service. The income comes from labour and tooling, not from yielding on €52.

Your most distinctive asset appears to be **credible rejection of misleading crypto performance claims**. Test whether anyone pays for that before spending another month finding another trade.

### B. Passive lending or delegated staking

These are genuinely different from the categories you listed. They can accommodate small capital on some networks, but they do not solve the income problem.

For scale, **a hypothetical 5% annual yield** on €52–300 is €2.60–15 annually, before costs and losses. Smart-contract, asset, depeg and custody risks remain.

So: missing category, yes. Meaningful small-capital business, generally no.

I would not send you into liquidation bots, keeper competition or elaborate funding-rate carry merely to produce another idea. Your constraints make their operational economics suspect.
