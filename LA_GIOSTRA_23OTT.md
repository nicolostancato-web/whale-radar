# The 53 SOL were a carousel — 23 Oct

**Question:** wallets receive tokens they never bought and sell them. Can we do it?

**Answer: there is nothing to copy, because there was no profit.**

## How the illusion was built, and how it fell

| step | what we saw | what it actually was |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a wallet banking ~53 SOL with no purchases | the sum of inflows only, on one address |
| 2 | gifts arriving through program `Locp…` | **Jupiter Lock**: one recipient per escrow, named by the creator. No way to enrol (confirmed independently by Grok from the program docs) |
| 3 | 28 recipients served once each, 12 distinct funders | **one operator, eight masks**: 28/28 pairs overlap, four at Jaccard 1.00 with up to 20 shared slots |
| 4 | full history of the recipient | **net zero SOL** over 405 signatures: +66.88 in from 56 market sales, −72.06 out |
| 5 | where the outflows go | **100% withdrawals**, 55 of 57 to one address |
| 6 | that address | +75.61 in, −75.61 out, the same two counterparties on both sides, 66 times each |

Three addresses passing the same SOL in a circle. Nothing is retained anywhere.

## The instrument gains a rule

A wallet's profit cannot be read on one address. **Follow the money until it
stops moving. If it never stops, there was no profit** — only a carousel that
shows revenue to whichever address you happen to open.

Corollary already in force: the counterparty identifies the event. An outflow to
the market is a purchase; an outflow to an address is a transfer, and a transfer
is not a cost.
