There is a third discriminator besides capital and speed: **producing something valuable that competitors cannot cheaply reproduce**. That includes information, computation, security discoveries, and services. It is not another trading setup. It changes what you are selling.

I take your measurements as given below. Thresholds calculated from them are conditional on those measurements, not independent estimates of current markets.

## 1. Categories where capital and speed are not the primary discriminator

First, an important distinction:

- **What determines whether you have an edge?**
- **What determines how much money that edge can earn?**

Information can determine the first while capital still determines the second. A category can escape the speed race without escaping the bankroll constraint.

| Category | Who pays, and why? | Actual discriminator | Constraint relevant to you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Information-driven pricing**: prediction markets, longer-horizon relative value, fundamental or event-driven positions | Counterparties with worse estimates, hedging needs, or nonfinancial preferences. Contractual settlement enforces the eventual payoff; nobody is compelled to offer you a mispriced trade. | Better calibrated probabilities or valuations—not necessarily faster reactions. | Still requires risk capital for trading income. Information can instead be sold, but then the business is research or software. |
| **Optimization paid by solution quality**: genuinely scored batch auctions, routing, portfolio/netting optimization, some solver markets | Users or protocols pay from execution improvements or a defined reward budget. Payment is enforceable when the mechanism commits to it. | Finding a better feasible solution before a deadline, rather than being first. | Must verify that the mechanism actually rewards quality; many nominal “solver” businesses are really inventory and latency businesses. Your UniswapX result also shows that a valid mechanism can have inadequate capacity. |
| **Security discovery and verification** | Protocols purchase vulnerability reports, audits, formal verification, and sometimes proofs. Payment follows a contract or bounty policy. | Finding and demonstrating a defect, or establishing a property others have not. | Low financial capital is possible; expertise and work are substantial. Bounties can be discretionary or disputed. Initially this is lumpy labor income, not autonomous compounding. |
| **Useful computation or differentiated infrastructure** | Protocols and users purchase proofs, data, indexing, monitoring, automation, or other outputs. They need the function, though they can switch suppliers. | Correctness, specialized implementation, reliability, proprietary data, or integration. | Commodity computation can revert to a hardware-capital competition. Commodity keepers can revert to speed. A laptop is not automatically competitive. |
| **Creation and distribution of an on-chain product** | Users pay recurring fees for software, risk management, vault logic, integrations, or other useful functionality. | Product quality, trust, distribution, and retention. | This can scale beyond personal trading capital, but it is a business. Demand must be established; settlement on-chain is not itself an advantage. |
| **Contribution-based rewards, grants, and competitions** | A treasury purchases ecosystem growth, research, implementation, or participation. | Eligibility, demonstrable contribution, or judged quality. | Budgets are finite, renewal is uncertain, and payment is often discretionary. Usually unsuitable as the foundation of a compounding machine. |

**There is no general category in which the payer “cannot avoid paying” merely because you participate.** The strong version exists only after a binding commitment: an escrowed contract, enforceable settlement rule, or accepted service agreement. Before that, counterparties can decline to trade, users can leave, and treasuries can stop subsidies.

The meaningful escape from your wall is therefore:

> **Become the supplier of a scarce output, rather than another claimant on a publicly visible spread.**

That does not establish that you possess such an output. It identifies where the wall is not structural.

## 2. What your search has—and has not—covered

Your fifteen roads are not fifteen independent tests of all possible income mechanisms. Many probe the same underlying structure:

> Public order flow or public price discrepancies, contested by participants with cheaper execution, better selection, or more inventory.

That is enough to reject those implementations. It is not enough to reject every market structure.

### Whole structures you have barely touched or not touched

| Market structure | What differs from the roads you tested | Does it escape your constraints? |
|---|---|---|
| **Central-limit-order books and derivatives** | Explicit bid/ask selection, queue position, funding, expiry, margin, and hedging. | Ordinary market making mostly restores the capital/speed wall. Longer-horizon relative value might be information-driven, but requires collateral and survives only if net carry exceeds all risks and costs. |
| **Prediction and contingent-claim markets** | Payout is tied to an external event or adjudicated outcome, not merely resale to another token buyer. | Potentially an information discriminator. Capital lockup, resolution risk, spreads, and limited capacity remain. |
| **Options and other nonlinear claims** | Prices incorporate volatility, tails, correlation, and path dependence. | Model or risk-estimation skill can matter. Selling tail exposure is not free income; collateral and rare losses can dominate the apparent edge. |
| **RFQ, dealer, and negotiated OTC markets** | Prices depend on client flow, inventory matching, and willingness to warehouse risk. | Usually inaccessible without inventory, relationships, or distribution—three things you explicitly lack. |
| **Batch and combinatorial auctions** | Participants may compete on the quality of a complete allocation, not just arrival time. | A genuine possible escape if quality earns payment and funding can be sourced. Requires mechanism-level evidence and a sufficiently large reward pool. |
| **Credit origination, underwriting, and distressed claims** | Information about borrowers, collateral, recovery, or legal enforceability matters. | Underwriting skill is distinct from speed. Lending itself remains capital-bound; earning origination or servicing fees is a service business. |
| **Protocol labor and resource markets** | Payment is for security, computation, data, or maintenance rather than a trading position. | The clearest structural escape from needing a large trading bankroll. Whether it can become a machine depends on repeatable demand and automation. |

A key distinction: **an untested structure is not an untested edge**. “Look at prediction markets” is not a conclusion supported by your evidence. It is only a statement about the scope of what you have ruled out.

Likewise, compensation for illiquidity, default risk, depegs, or short volatility does not refute your wall. Those are generally **capital earning a risk premium**, sometimes with losses hidden outside the observation window.

## 3. At what capital do the closed roads reopen?

For several roads, the honest threshold is **no capital-only reopening**. For others, your data do not identify a threshold. Supplying a precise number in those cases would invent the missing edge.

### The governing calculation

If an opportunity has:

- executable return \(e\), after proportional costs but before fixed costs;
- fixed cash cost \(F\);
- ticket size \(K\);

then:

\[
\text{net profit}=Ke-F
\]

and the fixed-cost break-even threshold is:

\[
K_{\mathrm{BE}}=\frac{F}{e}, \qquad e>0.
\]

If \(e\leq0\), increasing capital does not repair it. And if \(e\) falls with size, the simple threshold may not be executable.

### The stablecoin-arbitrage threshold

Your quoted gas toll on EUR 52 implies cash costs of:

\[
F=52\times(0.02\%\text{ to }0.1\%)
  =€0.0104\text{ to }€0.052.
\]

The median gap is:

\[
0.0009\%=0.000009.
\]

Consequently:

\[
K_{\mathrm{BE}}
=\frac{€0.0104\text{ to }€0.052}{0.000009}
\approx €1,156\text{ to }€5,778.
\]

**Those numbers cover only the stated gas toll.** They assume it is the total fixed cost of the operation and stays roughly constant.

If the quoted gap is before pool fees, those must first be subtracted. Impact, failed attempts, and the probability that the gap survives until execution also matter. If those eliminate the executable edge, the threshold disappears.

This is the one road where your numbers permit a concrete *fixed-cost* threshold—not a claim that savings of EUR 6,000 would make the strategy profitable.

## 4. The uncomfortable answer

**Yes. There is an honest reading in which continuing to search these same public trading mechanisms for a EUR 52 → EUR 500/day machine is the error. That is the strongest operational conclusion supported by your evidence.**

But the universal claim needs narrowing:

> “Nobody without capital or speed can ever do this” is not established.

Someone could possess exceptional information, discover a temporary mispricing, or have a lucky outcome. Those possibilities do not constitute a reproducible business model.

The supportable statement is:

> **You have no demonstrated trading edge, several rejected ones, and no evidence that any surviving opportunity has both the required economics and capacity. You should not commit savings on the premise that another variation will reveal one.**

EUR 500/day on an unchanged EUR 52 bankroll is **961.5% net per day**. If the plan is instead to compound into a larger bankroll first, it must specify the growth horizon, feasible reinvestment, drawdown risk, and the capital at which capacity saturates. “Compounding” does not supply any of those missing quantities.
