## 1. Missing attributes you will regret

### First priority: retain the underlying ordered events

For every relevant transaction, retain:

- Block number, block hash, block timestamp.
- Transaction hash and transaction index.
- Log index; trace/call position when available.
- Raw token amounts, token addresses and decimals.
- Transaction sender, destination, receipt status, gas used, effective gas price, and any additional chain-specific fee.
- Raw relevant receipt logs, or a durable link to your own stored copy.

**Unlocks:** causal ordering, partial exits, latency replay, transaction-level attribution, and correction of classification mistakes without another extraction.

At approximately 0.093-second blocks, “seconds after graduation” is inadequate. Several blocks can have the same integer-second timestamp. Preserve block and transaction ordering even where wall-clock precision is unavailable. Do not invent subsecond event times from the average block interval.

### Other indispensable fields

| Missing field | Specific analysis it unlocks |

## 2. Separating trades from transfers: the cheapest reliable join

**Join on transaction hash, then reconcile transaction-level flows. Do not join on timestamp, amount similarity, or Swap sender.**

For Uniswap v4, the singleton architecture matters: swaps are identified by **pool ID in PoolManager events**. The pool is not generally an independent token-holding contract whose address you can use as a simple transfer counterparty.

### Recommended pipeline

#### A. Build the verified pool registry

For each graduate, retain:

- PoolManager address and pool ID.
- Currency0/currency1, fee, tick spacing and hook.
- Token and quote asset.
- Exact graduation transaction and event boundary.

Verify the hook against the pool’s configuration. A shared hook alone is not a unique pool identifier.

#### B. Gather both swap events and token transfers

Collect:

1. PoolManager `Swap` events for the relevant pool IDs.
2. Target-token `Transfer` events.
3. Relevant liquidity events.
4. Full receipts and transaction envelopes for the union of candidate transaction hashes.

**Do not seed exclusively from target-token transfers.** V4 settlement/netting and internal balance mechanisms can make transfer activity an incomplete representation of swap activity.

Deduplicate transaction fetches across coins. One receipt can explain several relevant events.

#### C. Construct a flow ledger within each transaction

Decode:

- Target-token transfers.
- Relevant ERC-20 quote transfers.
- Wrap/unwrap events.
- All pertinent swap legs.
- Liquidity changes and known router events.

From the swap’s signed currency deltas, identify pool-level direction and amounts. From settlement flows and decoded route information, identify the payer and recipient.

For a simple, single-user swap with unambiguous token and quote flows, this is usually enough. For native settlement, router refunds, multi-user transactions and complicated routes, it may not be.

**Pool deltas are not automatically the trader’s all-in spend or receipt.** Hook fees, router fees, taxes, refunds and other legs can create differences.

#### D. Trace only the ambiguous cases

Escalate to call traces when receipts cannot determine:

- Internal native transfers and refunds.
- Which nested call produced which swap.
- The economic user behind a smart account or relayer.
- Multiple users or routes settled together.
- Hook-specific payments or accounting.

`tx.from` is a useful candidate, **not a universal trader identity**. It can be a bundler or executor. Likewise, token recipient alone does not identify who paid.

Use explicit classes:

- Confirmed user buy.
- Confirmed user sell.
- Mixed-route / arbitrage transaction.
- LP-related.
- Non-trade transfer.
- Unresolved.

Store the attribution rule/version and confidence.

**Do not force unresolved transactions into buys or sells.** Report unresolved coverage by volume, apparent profit and wallet type—not just event count. If the apparent winners overwhelmingly require unresolved attribution, the study has not identified winners.

### Important limitation

V4 can net settlement across several operations. There may be no unique one-to-one match between each Swap and each Transfer. In those cases, reconstruct the transaction or call-level route; do not manufacture an event-pair join.

**Cheapest reliable approach:** receipts and flow reconciliation for straightforward transactions, targeted traces for ambiguity, and an explicitly unscored residual. Neither “trace everything” nor “assume the sender is the user” is necessary.

## 3. How this study will produce a false positive

### The most likely mechanism: retrospective winner selection

You rank 62,234 wallets using the full 65 days, choose those with excellent outcomes, and then simulate following their trades within those same 65 days.

That will find “skill” even if much of the ranking is luck. A handful of extraordinary coins, correlated wallets and strategy tuning make it worse.

**Guard before analysis:**

1. Freeze chronological training, validation and untouched test periods.
2. Select and rank wallets using only information available before each simulated decision.
3. Freeze minimum history, eligibility rules, position sizing, entry/exit rules and latency assumptions before opening the test.
4. Never evaluate a follower on the trades that established its leader’s reputation.
5. Report dependence on individual coins, wallets/entities and days. Trade count is not independent sample size.

A first training-only diagnostics phase followed by a frozen chronological test is simpler and safer than extensive tuning across the whole dataset.

### Other concrete false positives

**Free inventory looks like trading skill.**  
A creator-related wallet receives tokens, sells them after graduation, and becomes your best “trader.”

**Guard:** opening inventory and provenance; separate returns on post-graduation purchased inventory. Unknown cost basis is not a trading win.

**Same-transaction arbitrage looks copyable.**  
A bot buys and sells atomically. Its first buy log appears attractive, but by the time you observe it, the position is closed.

**Guard:** make decisions only after the full observable transaction; classify atomic round trips separately.

**A price mark looks like an exit.**  
The simulator values inventory at the last print or the price that triggered a sell, despite insufficient executable liquidity.

**Guard:** execute against state available after the exit decision and your delay, with size, fees and slippage. If execution fails, retain inventory and record failure.

**Address proliferation looks like repeated independent skill.**  
One operator splits a strategy among many wallets.

**Guard:** evaluate concentration and uncertainty at coin/day/entity levels where defensible. Treat entity clusters as hypotheses, not ground truth.

### Fix the open-position accounting now

Keep separate:

- Realised P&L under a stated inventory-accounting convention.
- Remaining inventory and remaining cost basis.
- Estimated net liquidation value at a defined cutoff and position size.
- A zero-value residual stress case.

For transferred inventory, carry provenance where possible or mark basis unresolved. **“Never exited = −100%” must not be your primary profit field.** It is neither economically accurate for liquid inventory nor sufficient protection against basis mistakes.

## 4. The post-graduation equivalent of the one-second trap

**The leader’s profit exists, but the observable signal is the transaction that consumes it.**

Examples:

- Their purchase moves a thin pool before you can react.
- They buy before a burst of demand; you see the purchase only after repricing.
- They arbitrage a discrepancy that their own transaction eliminates.
- They use private information, privileged inventory or a route you cannot reproduce.
- They exit before your entry arrives.
- Their reported returns require transaction ordering or execution costs unavailable to you.

A continuous market does not imply a persistent, copyable edge.

### Day-one measurement: the executable delay-and-size curve

For confirmed directional purchases, calculate follower returns at:

- Earliest state after the complete leader transaction—**a diagnostic upper bound**, not a claim of attainable execution.
- Your measured end-to-end latency.
- Slower latency quantiles, including tail latency.
- Several intended position sizes.

Apply equivalent latency to exits. Include fees, gas, price impact, failed execution and unresolved inventory.

Measure two distinct things:

1. **Entry deterioration:** how much worse is the executable entry after the delay?
2. **Complete follower P&L:** what happens under a fully specified observable exit rule?

Entry markouts alone cannot establish profitability.

Start a live observer immediately and record:

`event first received → decision → transaction submitted → transaction included`

Historical block timestamps cannot reconstruct RPC delivery delay. At this block rate, one second spans roughly eleven blocks; the distribution of inclusion delay matters, not just its mean.

Also report:

- Fraction of leader positions already closed before follower entry.
- Same-transaction and same-block round-trip rates.
- Leader profit versus delayed follower profit.
- Net follower return versus capital size.

**If the edge survives only at the leader’s execution price, the post-graduation thesis has reproduced your previous failure.**

## 5. First week: three measurements, in order

### 1. Profit reconciliation and strategy decomposition

**Question:** Are the apparent winners actually making money from ordinary post-graduation directional trades?

On a broad sample, plus the biggest apparent winners:

- Reconcile inventory against balances.
- Audit quote/native settlement.
- Separate fresh post-graduation purchases, legacy inventory liquidation, LP activity, arbitrage and unresolved flows.
- Measure profit concentration by coin, entity and transaction type.
- Inspect representative winning and losing transactions manually.

**Stop or redesign if:** balances/flows cannot be reconciled, or apparent profits mostly come from unknown basis and misattribution.

**Abandon ordinary wallet-copying early if:** essentially all genuine profit comes from activities that cannot be replicated by a delayed directional follower.

### 2. Measured-latency, realistic-size copyability

**Question:** Is there even an execution window worth pursuing?

Using a training-period candidate set selected without future information, replay complete copy rules at realistic delay and size.

Include an optimistic executable benchmark as an upper bound, but keep it separate from the deployable result.

**Abandon if:** even favourable, causally valid execution at your latency is consistently net negative, or positive returns exist only at negligible capacity or unattainable ordering.

This is the fastest test of the founder’s economic premise.

### 3. Forward persistence of wallet selection

**Question:** Can yesterday’s information identify tomorrow’s copyable winners?

Freeze one or a very small number of selection rules. Rank wallets on prior history, then test on later activity. Compare against simple baselines with similar activity and opportunity sets.

Report:

- Net deployable return and drawdown.
- Number of genuinely independent coin/entity/day outcomes.
- Concentration in the largest winners.
- Leader profit versus follower profit.
- Sensitivity to latency and size.

**Abandon or decline deployment if:** historical winners do not predict positive delayed-copy returns, or performance depends on a few coins or one related-wallet cluster. An inconclusive one-week result is not proof that no edge exists—but it is no justification for scaling the study or trading capital.
