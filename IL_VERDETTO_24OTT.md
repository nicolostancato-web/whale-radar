# The verdict, from two independent reviewers — 24 Oct

Astra and Grok were given the same dossier — fifteen measured closures, our
constraints, and the deliberately uncomfortable question — and answered
separately. They converge on the same arithmetic.

## The number that settles the frame

> **EUR 500/day on EUR 52 is 962% per day.**
> The best capital-proportional rate we measured — Base's 40 busiest pools —
> pays **0.0635% per day**.
> A factor of about **15,000**.

Any income that is *a share of what you post* cannot reach the target from this
bankroll, for any strategy, ever. All fifteen roads were of that kind.

## Thresholds, from our own data

| | capital required |
|---|---|
| EUR 10/day gross, LP on Base | ~EUR 15,700 |
| EUR 500/day gross, LP on Base | ~**EUR 787,000** |
| EUR 500/day, perpetual funding at a hot 0.3%/day | ~EUR 167,000 |
| EUR 500/day, tokenised T-bills at 4%/yr | ~EUR 4.6M |

And the roads closed **at a loss do not reopen with more money**: a negative
percentage times a larger ticket is a larger loss. Grok's table says "nowhere"
for eleven of the fifteen.

## The three doors where the discriminator is neither capital nor speed

**1. Be the address the contract names.** The protocol, the hook, the creator,
the app that routed the flow. Whoever uses that contract **cannot not pay** —
the deduction sits in the same transaction as the thing they came for. They can
refuse the venue; they cannot use it for free. pump.fun collected **USD 1.98M in
one day** this way, from the same population we measured losing 5–7% as buyers.
The right is acquired by shipping the contract and having flow go through it.

**2. A judgment still true an hour later.** Prediction markets: you win by
calibration, not by milliseconds. But the euros stay capped by capital — EUR 52
on a ten-point edge returns about EUR 10.

**3. Work a buyer has already agreed to pay for.** Bounties, audits, datasets,
operated services. Labour income, lumpy, not a compounding machine by itself.

Everything else collapses back into the two rules. Flash loans remove the
capital requirement and leave a pure speed auction — Jito's tip stream, the
price searchers pay to win, was **USD 139,017 in 24 hours**.

## The uncomfortable answer, which both gave

**Yes.** Continuing to search public trading mechanisms for a EUR 52 → EUR
500/day machine is the error. Not because crypto does not pay, but because it
pays **whoever collects the toll**, and we spent three months on the side that
pays it.

## What would make us believe the opposite

Four conditions, all of them, on a sample this instrument has not touched:

1. a payer named in advance whose daily pie is at least EUR 500, shared out
   neither by capital nor by a latency auction;
2. a rule frozen before the outcomes, whose **lower bound** after every toll is
   at least EUR 500/day with the bankroll capped at EUR 52;
3. the same euros when a second instance runs the same rule — if the second
   halves the first, it was a queue, and we have measured queues;
4. survival of the four specific failures that killed this week's positives: no
   depth estimated from future swaps, no fee substituted when the contract
   refuses to answer, no wallet ranked after its career is known, no transfer
   between our own addresses counted as income.

A positive backtest is not that evidence. The archive already contains those —
and the autopsies.
